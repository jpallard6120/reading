# The Forever War

## Verdict

Unscored. A page-turner with huge action and convincing era-to-era social change, but also one of the strangest recent reads: several social extrapolations and the final resolution were hard to swallow even while the book remained compelling.

## What worked

- Strong page-turning momentum
- Intense action
- Distinct historical eras that felt meaningfully different
- Wider-world changes across relativistic time were vivid and believable
- Memorable enough to be worth reading despite major objections

## What did not

- The army-mandated sexual arrangements were deeply off-putting
- The later society-wide shift to homosexuality felt bizarre and difficult to accept
- The ending reduced the enormous war to a failure of communication in a way that felt too convenient
- The reason communication had not happened earlier felt hokey

## Reader comments

## Recommendation implications

### Recommend more of

- Relativistic or long-timescale stories where social change is made concrete
- Action-heavy science fiction with real conceptual consequences
- Books interesting enough to remain worthwhile despite discomfort or ambivalence

### Do not assume

- A page-turner automatically earns a high rating
- Ambivalence needs to be flattened into a numerical score
- Bold social extrapolation is valuable when it feels arbitrary or dated rather than earned

### Largest risk in similar books

- Provocative social speculation or a too-neat thematic ending can undermine otherwise excellent action and worldbuilding.
