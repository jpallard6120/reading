# Usurpation

## Verdict

The weakest book of the trilogy. Fine, but not great. The story became too fuzzy and spread across too many factions, characters, and threads to achieve the same level of immersion as the first two books.

## What worked

- Continued access to the `Semiosis` universe
- Enough interesting material to remain worthwhile
- The broader universe still contains strong ideas and missed possibilities

## What did not

- Too much happening at once
- Different factions felt less believable and less developed
- Too many characters lacked enough development to remain memorable
- Character deaths could land with little emotional weight because it was difficult to remember who the character was
- The expansion in scope diluted the depth that made `Semiosis` so strong
- Pax deserved more time
- Other potential settings could have supported deeper stories of their own
- Nave station felt like a major missed opportunity because the story never really explored it

## Recommendation implications

### Recommend more of

- Depth over breadth
- Smaller numbers of well-developed factions and characters
- Returning to richly established places when they still contain unexplored depth
- New locations that receive enough narrative space to become real places rather than names on a map

### Avoid

- Expanding scope faster than characterization and worldbuilding can support it
- Large casts where individual characters become interchangeable or forgettable
- Interesting settings introduced as possibilities but barely explored
- Breadth that dilutes emotional investment
