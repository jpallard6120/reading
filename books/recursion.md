# Recursion

## Verdict

A solid 9/10. More Hollywood blockbuster than fast food: slick, spectacular, emotionally believable, and built around a convoluted world that still made sense on its own terms.

## What worked

- High-concept story with strong momentum
- Repeated apocalyptic sequences created vivid spectacle
- The speculative world remained coherent despite becoming increasingly complicated
- Characters evolved in believable ways
- The book balanced blockbuster entertainment with enough substance to avoid feeling shallow

## What did not

- No major objection was recorded

## Reader comments

## Recommendation implications

### Recommend more of

- Propulsive high-concept thrillers
- Spectacular escalation supported by coherent internal rules
- Characters who change plausibly under extraordinary pressure
- Blockbuster-scale stories that retain emotional and conceptual substance

### Do not assume

- Fast pacing makes a book shallow
- Convoluted speculative mechanics are a problem when the reader remains oriented
- A cinematic style is equivalent to fast-food fiction

### Largest risk in similar books

- Spectacle replacing character development or internal coherence
