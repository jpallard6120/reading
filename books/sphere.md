# Sphere

## Verdict

7/10. An enjoyable underwater techno-thriller with a strong setting, but the speculative mechanism drifted too far toward magic, and the characters felt clichéd and difficult to relate to.

## What worked

- Interesting underwater setting
- The setup felt like an interplanetary adventure transplanted beneath the ocean
- Thriller pacing remained enjoyable

## What did not

- Suspension of disbelief weakened as the premise became more magical than concrete
- The "think something and it happens" mechanism lacked a satisfying basis
- Characters felt unrelatable and clichéd
- The book never hit especially hard despite being perfectly readable

## Reader comments

> Interesting setting, felt like "interplanetary adventure, but underwater". But again, like Jurassic Park, I had more trouble with my suspension of disbelief. It went a bit into the magical / fantastic without any concrete basis (think something and it happens.... But why?). I also found the characters unrelatable and kinda cliche. Maybe I just don't like Crichton's style in general? It wasn't unenjoyable, it was a nice read and the thriller aspect was fine, but it just didn't hit super hard in general.
>
> 7/10

## Recommendation implications

### Recommend more of

- Contained techno-thrillers with mechanisms that remain concrete and explicable
- Strong settings supported by equally strong characterization

### Do not assume

- An interesting scientific setting can compensate for hand-waved speculative mechanics
- Michael Crichton's thriller style is automatically a strong fit

### Largest risk in similar books

- A premise that crosses from speculative science into unexplained wish-fulfillment can break immersion even when the thriller engine works.
