# Semiosis

## Verdict

Excellent. A deeply built world in which ecology, society, and character choices are tightly connected. The alien environment is not scenery; it is an active system that constrains, negotiates with, and reshapes the human society living inside it.

## What worked

- Deep, consequential worldbuilding
- Difficult and unusual environmental constraints that consistently make sense
- Choices with real consequences inside those constraints
- Multiple deep characters living inside a complex social world
- Intelligent and resourceful people solving difficult problems together
- Plant-to-plant communication that felt coherent, imaginative, and earned
- Plants with distinct personalities rather than generic alien intelligence
- Ecological interaction as politics: negotiation, competition, deception, and backstabbing happening through the living environment itself
- Nonhuman intelligence that remains genuinely nonhuman while still becoming understandable and compelling

## What did not

- No major objection was recorded

## Recommendation implications

### Recommend more of

- Worlds where ecology is an active participant in the story rather than background scenery
- Alien environments whose constraints visibly shape human society and decision-making
- Multiple intelligent actors with competing incentives, including nonhuman actors
- Complex social systems inhabited by resourceful, believable characters
- Nonhuman intelligence grounded in the organism's own biology and mode of existence
- Speculative settings where every strange element has practical and social consequences

### Avoid assuming

- Intelligent nonhuman characters need familiar bodies, language, or social structures to become emotionally legible
- Worldbuilding must be technologically driven; ecology and biology can provide equally rich causal systems

## Context

This was one of the clearest matches yet for the preference for causal worldbuilding. The environment, human society, and nonhuman intelligence all affect one another rather than existing as separate layers.
