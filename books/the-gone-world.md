# The Gone World

## Verdict

Great. The time-travel system felt original and internally coherent, the crime scenes were gruesomely visceral, and the horror was used deliberately rather than for its own sake.

## What worked

- The echo and IFT concepts created a form of time travel that avoids contradictions
- The speculative rules were unusual but understandable
- Crime-scene descriptions were vivid and viscerally effective
- Horror appeared where the story earned it
- The horror supported the mystery and stakes instead of becoming the point of the book

## What did not

- No major objection was recorded

## Reader comments

> The Gone World was GREAT.
>
> I loved the original take on time travel, how it doesn't allow contradictions through the "echos" and "IFTs" concepts. The crime scene descriptions were gruesomely visceral. Horror isn't a style I'm attracted to in general, but this one was absolutely well done, horror was put in with thought and in the right places, it wasn't horror for horrors sake.

## Recommendation implications

### Recommend more of

- High-concept thrillers with clear and internally coherent speculative rules
- Original treatments of time travel
- Dark material that is purposeful and structurally important
- Familiar worlds that expand into stranger ideas without losing orientation

### Do not assume

- Horror is a poor fit when it is used carefully
- Graphic material is unwelcome when it earns its place
- Time-travel stories must rely on contradiction or paradox

### Largest risk in similar books

- Horror or violence becoming decorative rather than necessary to the story
